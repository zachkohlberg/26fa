CSC 111 Data Structures and Abstractions

Grading

The grading system for my classes aims to be simple and transparent, and I try to incorporate some ideas from labor-based grading. It works as follows:

  • Larger assignments are broken into tasks:
    • Each task has a point value and is marked complete (you get 100% of the task’s points) or incomplete (you get 0 points for the task) when your work is graded. There is no partial credit for incomplete tasks.
    • Each task will list one or more requirements that must be met for the task to be considered complete. If you meet the requirements, then the task is complete. If not, then the task is incomplete.
    • If revisions or multiple attempts are allowed, then each attempt will receive a separate score. Only the highest-scoring attempt for an assignment counts towards your grade.
  • There may be smaller assignments that award points if they are completed, or assignments that reward points for some ongoing obligation (such as weekly practice exercises or class participation).
  • Your final grade is the sum (not the average) of your assignment grades and uses the letter grade scale in QU’s grading system. This means five points on any assignment is equal to five points on your final grade.

My goal is to emphasize the work you put in and how much you engage in activities that will help you learn the material rather than the results of this work.

Deadlines

Unless otherwise noted, all deadlines are by 11:59 PM Eastern Time on the given date. Late work is not accepted.

Larger assignments that allow multiple submissions will be graded on Mondays and Fridays. You may submit these assignments as often as you want until the deadline. Expect to revise these assignments at least two or three times before receiving full credit, and make your first submission at least two weeks before the deadline to allow time for revisions.

Extensions

If you would like extra time to work on an assignment, then you can request an extension to the deadline via email. When requesting an extension:

  • Tell me why you want the extra time. You don’t need to go into detail if the reasons relate to something private. It’s fine to just say you’re dealing with a personal, medical, or family issue.
  • Tell me how much additional time you need.
    • If you email me at least one week before the deadline, then I will allow extensions of up to two weeks.
    • If you email me less than one week before the deadline, then you can at most double the amount of time remaining. For example, if you email me two days before the deadline, then I will allow at most two extra days.
    • If you email me within twenty-four hours of the deadline or after the deadline, then it’s too late to receive an extension.
    • Regardless of timing, I will not allow extensions beyond the end of finals week.

If you are in a situation that requires a longer extension than allowed above, then you will need to email the OSA (access@qu.edu) about an accommodation. See the Accessibility and Support Policy for more information.

Submissions

Unless otherwise noted in the assignment, all work must be submitted on Canvas using the submission link for that assignment. When submitting an assignment:

  • Include all required files to the submission.
  • When submitting code, attach each of your source code (.java) files to the same submission.
  • Ensure all files are named correctly. Capitalization matters!
  • Unless the assignment specifies a package structure, your java files must not declare a package.
  • Do not submit archives (zip files or tarballs) or links to file sharing sites (GitHub, Dropbox, OneDrive, etc.). You must attach each separate file to the same Canvas submission.
  • Do not include compiled programs (.class or .jar files) unless required by the assignment.

Submissions that do not follow these directions may receive a zero with little or no feedback.
